    0.6.0 release preparation: real-data comparisons¶

    0.6.0 makes permutation the default mAP null, including for multilabel APs. The implementations in #156 and #157 are merged; this page records the prepublication comparison. See the migration guide for API and input-lifecycle changes. Publication remains a separate action.

    Minimal summary: what happens to p-values?¶

    mAP stays the same; the comparison used to judge its significance changes.

    • Permutation versus joint: permutation retains the measured similarities; joint replaces them with random pair similarities, sharing within-group positive pairs. Real clustering and the permitted label moves can make permutation p-values either larger or smaller.
    • Copied with equal pair counts: members reuse the same random AP score, so adding replicates does not narrow its null. P-values can be larger than those from joint or permutation. With unequal counts, separate AP draws can instead make copied p-values much smaller.
    • Layout and precision matter: plate restrictions and overlapping labels determine the permitted comparisons. Near the FDR cutoff, small sampling differences can flip several decisions together.

    No method is uniformly more conservative, and a higher discovery count does not establish better calibration. These comparisons use all three methods in 0.6.0; copied represents the earlier statistical model, not exact reproduction of an older release's numbers.

    Small real examples¶

    Two groups illustrate opposite p-value changes under the copied model:

    Real group Pair-count pattern Observed mAP Permutation p Joint p Copied p
    LINCS compound BRD-K68132782-003-13-8 Six members, each with 5 positives among 29 candidates 0.4912 0.00726 0.00106 0.04725
    LINCS target PSMB1 Two members, each with 1 positive, among 49 and 50 candidates 1.0000 0.01068 0.00989 0.00039

    These are raw p-values from seed 0, selected to illustrate the two conditions. For the perfect PSMB1 pair, an independent calculation gives joint probability 1/98 = 0.010204 and copied probability 1/(49×50) = 0.000408, consistent with sampling. The copied null's different configurations are sampled separately; the joint null retains their shared positive pair.

    A still smaller explanation uses six real LINCS wells: compound wells E13 and E14 at two doses, and controls A01–A04. The treated wells find each other first, giving mAP 1. But controls A02 and A03 also form a perfect pair. Moving two labels over the unchanged measurements gives 15 possible assignments; exactly two achieve that perfect score. The exact permutation p-value is 2/15 = 0.133, versus 0.111 for joint and 0.200 for copied. All fifteen assignments and independent checks are included with the analysis.

    Scope and reproducibility¶

    These datasets appear in Kalinin et al. (2025). This is a workflow/model-sensitivity check, not reproduction of the published figures or a calibration experiment:

    • LINCS uses the example plate and pools doses; the paper used same-dose, cross-plate, sphered profiles. Target tests here include all 51 annotated compounds, unlike the earlier activity-selected smoke check.
    • Cell Health retains six wells per guide and cutting controls in each cell line. The plate-matched variant changes reference rules, so its mAP can differ from the pooled-control row; scores remain identical across methods within each row.
    • Matched Cell Painting/nELISA uses 300 shared compounds, harmonized by ID/count as in the paper. Target tests include all annotated compounds, with no activity-based selection. Singleton targets are untestable.
    • Permutation assumes exchangeability within its pools; normalization or connected multilabel components do not establish that assumption.
